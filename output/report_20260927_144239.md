## Executive Summary

The findings show that established memory assessments such as the Digit Span and Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT) have documented reliability, validity, and age‑specific normative data, while several free online platforms and mobile apps offer memory testing exercises. Randomized controlled trials evaluating the efficacy of free memory‑training apps exist, but the reported evidence is limited and often embedded within broader multi‑domain interventions. Consequently, while basic information on tools and some efficacy data are available, the evidence base is not comprehensive enough to definitively endorse any single free app or norm‑referenced protocol for clinical use.

## Key Findings

- Memory assessment tools mentioned include Digit Span, Word List Recall, Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT), and California Verbal Learning Test.  
- RAVLT (NIH Toolbox) involves three immediate recall trials of a 15‑word list and a delayed recall after 5–25 minutes.  
- A systematic review identified more than 20 studies (1994‑2011) on the Reliable Digit Span, noting limitations affecting clinical generalizability.  
- The DigiSpan computer‑based adaptive test provides normative and test‑retest reliability data for adults.  
- Normative data for RAVLT are available for age groups 6‑8, 9‑14, and 15‑64 (means, SDs, percentiles) and regression‑based norms for adults 49‑79 in Norwegian and Swedish samples.  
- Additional RAVLT norms include sex‑specific tables for males aged 8‑13 and age‑, gender‑, education‑adjusted cut‑scores from the Mayo Clinic Study of Aging.  
- Free online memory testing resources: Cognitive Train (short‑term, working, visual, spatial, auditory tests), “Best Free Brain Training Apps in 2026” list, and Free Brain Games Online (science‑based, no registration).  
- Randomized controlled trials involving free memory‑training apps:  
  - Protocol for the NeNaE mobile app study in mild cognitive impairment.  
  - RCT of the MeMo web app for neurocognitive disorders.  
  - Umbrella review of app‑based health interventions reporting efficacy data.  
  - RCTs of multi‑domain interventions (e.g., FINGER) that include cognitive training components.

## Comparison / Analysis

The documented tools (Digit Span, RAVLT) have a longer research history, with peer‑reviewed studies establishing their psychometric properties and providing age‑specific norms, which supports their use in formal assessment contexts. In contrast, free memory‑training apps are emerging and have been investigated mainly through pilot protocols or as components of broader interventions; direct evidence of their standalone efficacy remains sparse. The existence of normative data for RAVLT across multiple age brackets contrasts with the lack of comparable detailed norms for Digit Span within the presented findings. Overall, the findings suggest a solid foundation for traditional memory tests but a comparatively limited empirical base for free digital training tools.
